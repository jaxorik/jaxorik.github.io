FOR THE PEOPLE CLOSEST TO THE INCIDENT

Jaxorik is a founder-led company in Albuquerque, New Mexico. I'm building forecasting models that run without a connection and stay simple enough to audit.

Status: in development. Looking for pilot partners.

Why Jaxorik

I grew up in Los Alamos. In May 2000, when I was twelve, my family evacuated ahead of the Cerro Grande Fire. We watched it on the news, and the thing I remember most is a firefighter spraying water on his neighbor's house while his own burned to the ground.

I never forgot him. He's who I build for.

The Marines taught me how much equipment matters when the decision is life or death. Years later, training in land navigation with Cibola Search and Rescue, I used mapping tools that work with no connection and catch up later. I wanted forecasting to work the same way.

The physics-based fire models that crews rely on today are good. They can also be incomplete when conditions change faster than their inputs. So I'm building a second opinion: small models for chaotic problems like wildfire spread and grid failure, made to run alongside a physics model on the same tablet. They work without a connection, and they're simple enough that a person can inspect how a forecast was made. I'm building them so the person in the field can correct the model and record why.

The people closest to the fire usually know something the models don't. Jaxorik is built on that assumption.

How I Work

People decide

A forecast supports the person making the call. I'm building these models so someone in the field can correct one and record why.

Simple enough to audit

I use small models with transparent methods and reproducible results, so a person can inspect how a forecast was made.

Runs without a connection

The models are designed to run on a tablet or laptop in the field, with no cloud needed to get a forecast.

Messy data on purpose

Field data is incomplete and noisy. My research uses real gravitational wave detector data that includes corrupted and missing samples, instead of throwing those samples away.

Decision support only, never autonomous weapons. Built in the United States, with export-control rules in mind from the start.

For model documentation and validation results, including the gravitational wave tests, request the technical package. Solution details are on the Defense & Energy and Disaster Response pages.
